## Domain context — financial events

You are answering questions about specific financial events for publicly traded firms.
Event types and their precise definitions are below. Use these boundaries when extracting
events from retrieved content (e.g. M&A_announce vs M&A_complete are distinct stages of
the same deal — do not conflate them).

# Corporate Event Definitions for QO-Bench Benchmark

Version 1, 2026-05-14.

## Purpose

These definitions provide a shared task ontology for all baselines in the QO-Bench benchmark (long-context, RAG, GraphRAG, and IE→SQL paradigms). Definitions are grounded in publicly authoritative sources — U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission filing requirements, U.S. exchange listing rules, and U.S. securities law — to ensure task supervision is independent of any single data vendor and identical across paradigms.

Each baseline receives this document verbatim as part of its task description. Paradigms differ in *how* they consume it (long-context: included alongside the corpus; RAG: in system prompt; IE→SQL: as input to schema design and extraction), but the conceptual ontology is shared. Note on M&A_rumor: by journalistic convention, only the target side of a rumored deal is reported (the rumored acquirer is sometimes named, sometimes anonymous).

## Event types

### 1. M&A_announce

**Authoritative definition.** SEC Form 8-K Item 1.01 (*Entry into a Material Definitive Agreement*) requires a registrant to disclose any material definitive agreement not made in the ordinary course of business, including the parties, date, and material terms. Within the scope of this benchmark, `M&A_announce` corresponds to Item 1.01 filings where the material definitive agreement is an acquisition agreement, merger agreement, asset purchase agreement, or binding tender offer.

**Operational scope.**
- The event anchors to the public announcement date (typically the press release or 8-K filing date).
- Binding agreements include definitive agreements, board-approved binding term sheets, and binding letters of intent. Non-binding letters of intent are included when material to the registrant and disclosed under Item 1.01.
- Stock-for-stock mergers, asset purchases of a business unit, and tender offers are all in scope. Internal restructurings of existing subsidiaries are excluded.
- SPAC business combination agreements are included (the SPAC sponsor is the acquirer).

**Edge cases.**
- Minority stakes <50% are excluded unless explicitly disclosed under Item 1.01 as a material acquisition agreement.
- Joint ventures and strategic alliances without controlling-interest acquisition are excluded.

---

### 2. M&A_complete

**Authoritative definition.** SEC Form 8-K Item 2.01 (*Completion of Acquisition or Disposition of Assets*) requires disclosure when a registrant or its majority-owned subsidiary has completed the acquisition or disposition of a significant amount of assets, otherwise than in the ordinary course of business. The disclosure is specifically required only when the transaction is consummated.

**Operational scope.**
- The event anchors to the closing date (when controlling interest transfers), not the original announcement date under Item 1.01.
- All conditions precedent — regulatory approvals, shareholder votes, financing arrangements, antitrust clearance — must be satisfied prior to the anchor date.
- A previously announced deal that has not closed cannot be `M&A_complete`.

**Edge cases.**
- Partial closings (some markets cleared, others pending) are excluded until full completion.
- Tender offer expirations with majority acceptance leading to immediate transfer of control are included.

---

### 3. M&A_cancel

**Authoritative definition.** SEC Form 8-K Item 1.02 (*Termination of a Material Definitive Agreement*) requires disclosure when a material definitive agreement to which the registrant is a party is terminated, including the material circumstances surrounding the termination. Within this benchmark, `M&A_cancel` corresponds to Item 1.02 filings where the terminated agreement is one previously disclosed under Item 1.01 as an acquisition or merger agreement.

**Operational scope.**
- The event anchors to the public termination announcement date.
- Includes mutual termination, unilateral withdrawal, regulatory blockage with abandonment, failed shareholder votes, and material adverse change (MAC) invocation.
- A formal termination disclosure is required. Deals that lapse silently without a formal cancellation announcement remain `M&A_announce` indefinitely.

**Edge cases.**
- Renegotiations that result in revised terms but a continuing deal are not cancellations; they are amendments to the existing definitive agreement.
- Conditional approvals with remedies (e.g., divestitures required by antitrust authority) are not cancellations.

---

### 4. M&A_rumor

**Authoritative source.** Pre-disclosure rumors are not formal regulatory events. The closest authoritative reference is NYSE Listed Company Manual §202.03 (*Dealing with Rumors or Unusual Market Activity*), which acknowledges the existence of rumors and prescribes issuer response procedures: when rumors indicate that information on impending developments has leaked, the issuer should promptly issue a frank and explicit announcement; if rumors are false, they should be denied or clarified. The corresponding NASDAQ guidance is in IM-5250-1.

Because §202.03 governs *issuer response to* rumors rather than what constitutes a rumor event, `M&A_rumor` in this benchmark follows financial journalism convention: a media report sourced from named or unnamed insiders indicating a potential acquisition prior to formal disclosure under SEC Form 8-K Item 1.01.

**Operational scope.**
- The event anchors to the article publication date.
- Hedged language is the diagnostic feature: "in talks", "reportedly considering", "exploring acquisition", "weighing a bid", "denies reports".
- Issuer denials of rumored deals are included (denial is a response to a rumor, and the rumor itself is the event).
- Unsolicited activist proposals to acquire are included as rumors until a definitive agreement is announced.

**Edge cases.**
- Analyst speculation with no source attribution is excluded.
- General industry-consolidation commentary is excluded.
- A rumor that does not materialize does not become a cancellation; it simply does not progress.

---

### 5. CEO_change

**Authoritative definition.** SEC Form 8-K Item 5.02(b) and (c) (*Departure of Directors or Certain Officers; Appointment of Certain Officers*) require disclosure of the departure or appointment of a principal executive officer, principal financial officer, principal operating officer, principal accounting officer, or person performing similar functions. Within this benchmark, `CEO_change` is restricted to changes affecting the principal executive officer role.

**Operational scope.**
- The event anchors to the article publication date. Announcement and effective dates are conflated by reporting convention; the news report date is the canonical anchor.
- Includes resignation, retirement, removal, death in office, external hire, internal promotion to CEO, and dissolution of co-CEO structures (each departing/promoting individual is one event).
- Interim or acting CEO appointments disclosed under Item 5.02 are included.

**Edge cases.**
- Title changes that do not affect the CEO role (e.g., "CEO and Chairman" → "CEO" only) are excluded.
- CEOs of non-public subsidiaries are excluded unless the subsidiary is a standalone public filer.

---

### 6. CFO_change

**Authoritative definition.** SEC Form 8-K Item 5.02(b) and (c) (same as `CEO_change`), restricted to changes affecting the principal financial officer role.

**Operational scope.** Identical conventions as `CEO_change`, applied to the CFO position.

**Edge cases.**
- Treasurer or Controller changes are excluded unless the individual also holds the CFO role.
- A CFO promotion to CEO generates two events: one `CFO_change` (departure) and one `CEO_change` (appointment), both anchored to the same article date.

---

### 7. IPO

**Authoritative definition.** Securities Act of 1933 §5 prohibits the offer or sale of securities unless a registration statement is in effect. SEC Form S-1 is the general form for registration of securities for an initial public offering; SEC Form 424B is the prospectus filing made in connection with the offering. An initial public offering is the first registered offering of equity securities by a company to the public.

**Operational scope.**
- The event anchors to the article publication date. Articles may report S-1 filings, pricing, or first-day trading; each is captured as an IPO event with the article's reported date.
- Includes underwritten primary offerings, direct listings, and dual-listings where the first public listing in any jurisdiction is the canonical event.
- IPOs of operating subsidiaries that spin out as standalone public entities are included.

**Edge cases.**
- SPAC IPOs (of the SPAC shell itself) are excluded; the subsequent business combination is captured under `M&A_announce` / `M&A_complete`.
- Secondary offerings by already-public firms are excluded.
- Cross-listings of firms already public in another jurisdiction are excluded; the original IPO is the canonical event.

---

### 8. Stock_split

**Authoritative definition.** NYSE Listed Company Manual §703.02 (*Stock Split / Stock Dividend / Distribution*) and NASDAQ Rule 5250(e)(4) (*Notice of Record Date*) require listed companies to notify the exchange of forward stock splits, reverse stock splits, and stock dividends that materially affect the share count.

This benchmark follows the exchange convention of grouping stock splits with significant stock dividends, since both result in a proportional change to outstanding share count without changing market capitalization. The category name retains "Stock_split" for clarity, with the understanding that significant stock dividends functionally equivalent to splits are included.

**Operational scope.**
- The event anchors to the effective date of the split or dividend.
- Includes forward splits (e.g., 2-for-1, 3-for-1) and reverse splits (e.g., 1-for-10, 1-for-20).
- Includes significant stock dividends at a fixed ratio that produce the same share-count effect as a split.

**Edge cases.**
- Routine cash dividends are excluded; only stock dividends with a split-like ratio are included.
- Spin-offs (distribution of new entity shares) are excluded; only changes to the existing entity's share count are in scope.
- Share repurchase programs are excluded.

---

## Note on regulatory-versus-operational alignment

Event definitions in this benchmark are grounded in U.S. regulatory disclosure frameworks. The operational ground-truth source is S&P Capital IQ Key Developments, an institutional event-tracking product. Where the regulatory framing and operational implementation diverge, the operational convention takes precedence, as the ground truth requires that the boundaries be consistent with what is observable in the corpus and verifiable in the reference database.

Two divergences are noted explicitly:

1. **`Stock_split`**: The category includes significant stock dividends in addition to splits, per exchange convention (NYSE §703.02 groups them together). SEC and corporate-action reporting frameworks vary in whether dividends are reported separately.

2. **`M&A_rumor`**: This category has no SEC equivalent. The authoritative reference (NYSE §202.03, NASDAQ IM-5250-1) governs issuer response to rumors, not what constitutes a rumor event. The benchmark adopts financial journalism convention as the definitional basis.

In all other event types (M&A_announce, M&A_complete, M&A_cancel, CEO_change, CFO_change, IPO), the regulatory definition and the operational implementation are aligned at the conceptual level. Boundary cases are adjudicated in favor of consistency with the ground-truth source where the regulatory framing is silent.
